Insights

Ideas and guidance for modern AI, data and cloud teams

Practical perspectives from our engineering, data, and AI teams on the challenges we help enterprises solve.

AI & Generative AI

Building Enterprise AI Agents That Work With Your Business Data

Agents become valuable when they can reliably reach trusted data and act within clear guardrails.

Key considerations
  • Ground agents in governed, permission-aware data sources.
  • Give agents a narrow set of well-defined tools and actions.
  • Log reasoning steps and enforce approval for high-impact actions.
  • Start with a single high-value workflow before scaling.
AI & Generative AI

RAG vs Fine-Tuning: Choosing the Right Enterprise AI Architecture

Most enterprise use cases are best served by retrieval-augmented generation, with fine-tuning applied selectively.

Key considerations
  • Use RAG when knowledge changes often and citations matter.
  • Use fine-tuning to shape style or behavior, not to inject facts.
  • Combine both when a domain needs new behavior and fresh knowledge.
  • Evaluate with real tasks, not just generic benchmarks.
Architecture

Databricks vs Snowflake vs Microsoft Fabric

Each platform is strong; the right choice depends on your workloads, team, and existing ecosystem.

Key considerations
  • Databricks: engineering-heavy and ML workloads on the lakehouse.
  • Snowflake: governed warehousing and analytics with strong SQL tooling.
  • Fabric: tight integration across Microsoft analytics and Power BI.
  • Evaluate governance, cost model, and skills before committing.
Data Engineering

Modernizing Legacy ETL Pipelines

Modernization works best as an incremental migration, not a big-bang rewrite.

Key considerations
  • Inventory pipelines and rank by business criticality.
  • Introduce a governed lakehouse layer alongside legacy ETL.
  • Migrate in waves with validation and reconciliation at each step.
  • Retire legacy jobs only once downstream consumers are verified.
Data Engineering

Building a Modern Lakehouse Architecture

A layered lakehouse balances flexibility with the trust analytics teams need.

Key considerations
  • Use bronze, silver, and gold layers with clear ownership.
  • Adopt open table formats for portability and performance.
  • Enforce schema, quality checks, and lineage by default.
  • Serve BI, ML, and AI from the same governed data.
Data Governance

How to Prepare Enterprise Data for Generative AI

Generative AI is only as good as the data and access controls behind it.

Key considerations
  • Improve quality and consistency in the sources that matter most.
  • Apply permission-aware retrieval so users only see what they may.
  • Track lineage and freshness for every retrieved source.
  • Keep sensitive data out of ungoverned prompts.
Data Engineering

Reducing Databricks and Spark Costs

Most cost savings come from better data layout, smarter job design, and right-sized compute.

Key considerations
  • Compact small files and choose the right partitioning.
  • Optimize joins and avoid unnecessary shuffles.
  • Right-size clusters and use autoscaling with spot where safe.
  • Measure cost per pipeline and make it visible to owners.
Data Engineering

Designing Reliable Real-Time Data Pipelines

Reliability in streaming comes from explicit contracts and back-pressure handling.

Key considerations
  • Define schemas and compatibility rules up front.
  • Design for idempotency and exactly-once where it matters.
  • Monitor lag, throughput, and failure rates continuously.
  • Plan replay and recovery, not just happy-path flow.
Data Governance

Building an Enterprise Data Governance Strategy

Governance succeeds when it is practical, automated, and tied to business outcomes.

Key considerations
  • Start with clear ownership for critical data domains.
  • Automate quality, lineage, and access controls where possible.
  • Define policies people can follow, not just documents.
  • Measure adoption and data trust over time.
Cloud

Moving From Legacy Data Warehouses to the Cloud

A successful warehouse migration modernizes processes, not just platforms.

Key considerations
  • Assess workloads and rationalize what should move or retire.
  • Model data for the cloud instead of lifting and shifting as-is.
  • Automate ingestion, transformation, and testing.
  • Plan a phased cutover with parallel validation.
